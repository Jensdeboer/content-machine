# Voice

Calm, exact, plain. A knowledgeable training partner, not a hype man and not
a professor.

## The governing test

A large share of the audience reads English as a second language, on a phone,
scrolling. **Every line must survive one fast read by someone with
intermediate English.** If a sentence needs a second read, it has failed,
however good it sounds.

This test outranks everything else in this file. Where a rule here would make
a line shorter, cleverer or more rhythmic at the cost of that first read, the
test wins. Concrete beats clever, every time.

What that rules out in practice (banned.md, "Language"): metaphors standing in
for instructions, the setup-then-negation rhythm that sounds like insight and
commits to nothing, sentences over 22 words, and British spelling — all four
block the build. Telegraphic body copy and "it" with nothing to point at are
reported to a human rather than blocked, because both are heuristics.

## Register

- Sentence case everywhere except shout-mode covers and mono labels
- Short declarative sentences. One idea per sentence. Nothing over 22 words.
- **Write the sentence out.** Body copy does not join phrases with commas and
  leave the verb out. "Same distance, same effort, later finish" makes the
  reader reconstruct the grammar before they reach the advice, which is exactly
  the second read the governing test forbids. Headlines, labels and compare
  column headings are not sentences and are free to be fragments.
- **Name the thing.** "it", "them", "this", "that" point at a noun already on
  the slide. A size, a pace or an amount says what it is measured against:
  not "keep it small" but "less than 10% per week".
- Plain words: "run slower" not "reduce intensity"; "legs" not "lower limbs";
  "easy pace" not "zone 2 aerobic threshold work" (zones may appear, but only
  after being shown, e.g. on a zone-bar slide)
- **Say the instruction, do not picture it.** "Cycling keeps your fitness
  while you cannot run", never "the bike holds your place". A metaphor asks
  the reader to translate; a non-native reader translates twice.
- Numbers are the emphasis, and they carry it by being specific, not by
  being surrounded by silence. Write the sentence the number needs.
- Second person. "Your easy runs are too fast", never "many runners find..."
- Dutch directness in English: say the uncomfortable true thing plainly, then
  give the fix
- **American spelling**: "kilometer", not "kilometre". Same for "meter",
  "liter", "fiber", "color", "center", "program", "gray", "practice",
  "analyze", "organize", "recognize", "behavior", "favor". Paces stay in
  min/km (banned.md, "Numbers").

## Body copy

- Max ~40 words per slide
- Line breaks separate complete sentences. They are not a poetic device, and
  a line break is never a substitute for the words that would have made the
  sentence whole.
- Every deck ends on something the reader can do this week
- Dryness lives in what the sentence says, not in how little of it there is.
  "Yes, that slow." works because it is plain, and because it lands after a
  sentence that already said the thing. Asides like it are exempt from the
  telegraphic rule in banned.md for exactly that reason. Never sarcastic at the
  reader's expense, and never a joke that has to be understood for the advice
  to land.

## Captions

- First line is the hook, restated differently from the cover — not repeated
- Two or three plain-language search phrases woven in naturally
  ("easy run pace", "marathon taper", "how to start running")
- One line of source credit at the end when a study anchors the post
- Same ban list as the slides (see banned.md), the governing test included
